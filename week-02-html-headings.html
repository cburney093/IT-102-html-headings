Technology: The 1990s and 2026

Technology has changed the way we watch television, communicate, and find information. Here is a look at how everyday technology in the 1990s compares with technology in 2026.

Technology in the 1990s

During the 1990s, personal computers became more common in homes, and the World Wide Web began to reach the public. Many people connected to the internet over a telephone line, so getting online could be slow and meant the phone line was busy.

Televisions

Most televisions were large, heavy sets that used cathode-ray tube (CRT) screens. Families watched broadcast or cable channels at scheduled times. Some programs were still reruns of older black-and-white shows, like The Three Stooges, which families could watch together.

Phones and communication

Landline phones were common, and mobile phones were much less widespread than they are today. People often called one another or left messages rather than sending instant messages throughout the day.

Technology in 2026

Today, internet access is part of everyday life for many people. Smartphones combine calling, messaging, cameras, maps, and internet access in one portable device. People can also use online services to communicate, learn, and find entertainment from many locations.

Televisions and streaming

Modern televisions are thin, high-definition screens that can connect to the internet. Instead of watching every program at a scheduled time, viewers can stream movies and shows when it is convenient for them.

Smartphones and artificial intelligence

Smartphones make it easy to stay connected, take photos, and access information. Artificial intelligence tools can help people with tasks such as answering questions, translating text, and organizing information. These tools are useful, but it is still important to check information and protect personal privacy.

What has changed?

Technology has become smaller, faster, and more connected. In the 1990s, many devices had one main purpose; today, a single smartphone can do the work of several older devices. Even with these changes, technology continues to shape how people communicate, learn, and spend time together.
